# Copy Notes — Voice & Style Guide

**Owner:** research agent · **Compiled:** 2026-10-02
**Upstream:** `docs/research/facebook-intel.md` §2.12 (the shop's observed voice) ·
`AGENT-BRIEF.md` §5 (brief constraints) · `content/faqs.md` (the tone model)

> **This is the register for the website. The Facebook page's register is different — see
> §3. Do not mix them.**
>
> **The core insight, and it comes from EYG's own posts, not from a brand book:** this shop
> earns trust with **concrete specificity**, not adjectives. It writes *"Installed DIN80
> Amaron Jade AGM battery"*, *"Replaced Steering Rack Assembly"*, *"265/65R17 RADAR RENEGADE
> R/T+"*. It does not write "quality service you can trust" — **because every one of its
> 138 competitors does**, and they all sound identical. `competitors.md` §3
>
> **So the rule for every word on this site: name the exact part, the exact size, the exact
> number, the exact time. Then stop.** `risks-dos-donts.md` D9

## 1. Twelve phrases we use

**Every one of these is a promise the shop can actually keep.** If you cannot keep it, it
is not on this list.

| # | Phrase | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Open Monday to Saturday, 8 AM to 5 PM. Closed Sunday."** | ✅ Verified fact. **Nobody in Balanga publishes their hours.** Stated plainly, with no "approximately" and no "except holidays" we cannot honour. `facebook-intel.md` §2.2 |
| 2 | **"You will know the price before you leave the house."** | The entire positioning. A real promise, and the thing 138 competitors will not commit to |
| 3 | **"We stop and ask first."** | 🔴 The single most important sentence on the site. It is the anti-bait-and-switch promise, it is entirely true of an honest shop, and it is what a sceptical customer is actually looking for. `risks-dos-donts.md` §3.2 |
| 4 | **"We never add work you did not approve."** | Same promise, said directly. Cheapest trust device in the trade |
| 5 | **"A listed Michelin and BFGoodrich dealer."** | ✅ **Checkable by anyone, in ten seconds.** The most credible sentence EYG can write, and no local competitor can say it. `facebook-intel.md` §2.6 |
| 6 | **"We measure it in millimetres, not 'looks okay'."** | Specific, and it implies a written report. Sceptical customers trust measurements, not adjectives |
| 7 | **"We will tell you which one your car needs before we start."** | Aimed straight at the single most common complaint in the PH auto trade: *"nobody told me it was synthetic."* `services.md` §2.1 |
| 8 | **"That is a 'no', not a 'maybe'. Here is what we do cover."** | The honesty line. 🔑 **This is the most defensible sentence in the whole guide** — a short, honest *no* signals that EYG knows what it does, and no competitor can copy it without changing how they operate |
| 9 | **"Your old tyre comes back to you."** | Short, concrete, and it forecloses a whole category of dispute before it starts. Costs nothing |
| 10 | **"Book a bay. It takes a minute."** | `/book` is a real 4-step flow, not a fake. Short, and it respects the customer's time — which is the thing that is actually scarce in a stranded-driver scenario |
| 11 | **"We are on EGSA Fourlanes, Tuyo."** | The landmark, in the shop's own words. A driver who has never been to Balanga needs this more than a map pin. `facebook-intel.md` §2.2 |
| 12 | **"Ask us, and we will tell you honestly whether we can do it."** | Both a service offer and a promise not to waste a trip. **The highest-value line on the site for the AC and undercoating questions**, where EYG's honest answer may well be no |

## 2. Twelve phrases we never use

| # | ❌ Phrase | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Quality service you can trust"** | Every competitor says it, so it says nothing. It is the sound of a page with no information on it |
| 2 | **"Your one-stop automotive solution"** | Meaningless, and it implies a scope we cannot evidence. The verified 10-item service list says far more in the same space |
| 3 | **"Certified technicians"** | 🚫 **Unfalsifiable.** "Certified by whom?" has no answer. **There is no general Philippine mechanic-licensing scheme** — the real, nameable credential is **TESDA**, and it must come with the qualification and the year. `legal-compliance-ph.md` §6 |
| 4 | **"Authorised dealer"** | 🚫 A dealer-locator listing is a **dealer record**, not a franchise authorisation, and authorisation is the brand's word to give. `legal-compliance-ph.md` §1.2, `risks-dos-donts.md` N32 |
| 5 | **"Official distributor"** | 🚫 A supply-chain claim about a contractual relationship. Unverified for Amaron, and self-claimed only for the other brands. `facebook-intel.md` §2.7 |
| 6 | **"Genuine parts only"** | 🚫 Unfalsifiable by you and by the customer. **Say the part brand and model you fitted instead** — which is verifiable, and which is what the shop already does |
| 7 | **"Serving Balanga since 20XX"** | 🚫 **Four contradictory dates exist** and none is confirmed. Claim no year. `facebook-intel.md` §2.1, `risks-dos-donts.md` N23 |
| 8 | **"Rated 4.9 by our customers"** | 🚫 **No Google Business Profile exists, so no rating exists.** A fabricated rating is a Consumer Act exposure and it would emit invalid structured data. `risks-dos-donts.md` N16 |
| 9 | **"24/7 roadside assistance"** | 🚨 **Zero evidence it exists** — and it is a **safety** claim. `risks-dos-donts.md` §5 |
| 10 | **"Limited time only!" / "3 slots left!"** | 🚫 Fake urgency. **Shop capacity is modelled at 3 bays with a 12:00–13:00 break, so any scarcity number is checkable** — and in a 138-business market where EYG has 308 followers, checking is exactly what people do. `risks-dos-donts.md` §2 |
| 11 | **"Cheapest in Bataan" / "Sulit!"** | 🚫 Provably false the moment a competitor undercuts, and it converts the one advantage EYG has — transparency — back into a price race it cannot win against Motorlandia and 137 independents. `competitors.md` §4 |
| 12 | **"The only tyre shop on EGSA Fourlanes"** | 🚨 **FALSE, and specifically so.** **Jed-M Tires & Batteries & Service Center is registered at "4 Lanes Egsa, Tuyo"** — the same street. A demonstrably false claim about an identifiable local competitor is a defamation risk and a self-inflicted review disaster. `competitors.md` §1.1, `risks-dos-donts.md` N22 |

**One rule covers all twelve:** 🚫 **If the shop's own receptionist would be uncomfortable
being asked "prove that", the sentence does not ship.** `ux-benchmarks-auto-shop.md` §6.4

## 3. The shop's own voice, and why the website is not it

**Measured, from 8+ real posts** (`facebook-intel.md` §2.12):

| Facebook register | Website register |
|---|---|
| Heavy emoji as **structural** section breaks | **No emoji in headings or body copy.** 🔴 The Taglish/emoji register belongs in the FAQ and in social captions. The website is read by someone comparing EYG against a national chain, and on that comparison a polished register wins |
| `🖤💛 … 💛🖤` on every post — it is a **brand mark in emoji** | Keep it in social only. The website's brand mark is the actual logo |
| Ends lines with `!!` and a `🔥` | No exclamation marks in website body copy. **One** in a CTA is plenty |
| Title Case service names | ✅ **Keep this on the website** — it is the shop's vocabulary and it is the SEO term |
| Action verbs, past tense, for completed work | ✅ **Keep this.** "Replaced Steering Rack Assembly", not "Steering Rack Replacement Services" |
| Exact tyre sizes: `265/65R17`, `11R22.5`, `185/65-14` | ✅ **Keep this — it is the single most persuasive habit the shop has.** Specificity beats adjectives |
| "Visit us in-store or message us to inquire" | ✅ **The same invitation, better written:** "Call, or book a bay on this page" |
| Fluent English throughout, no Taglish | The website adds **light** Taglish — in the FAQ and in headings, never in legal or technical copy. See §4 |

> 🔑 **The continuity to protect:** a customer who follows EYG from Facebook to the website
> should recognise the same business. That comes from **the same service names, the same
> voice of specificity, and the same landmark** — not from copying the emoji.

## 4. How we refer to customers

### "You." Never "the customer"

| ❌ | ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The customer will be charged" | "You'll be charged" | "The customer" makes the reader an outsider. They are the customer |
| "Please bring your vehicle" | "Bring the car" | Shorter, warmer, less bureaucratic |
| "Kindly bring" | "Bring" | **"Kindly" is a formal register that does not match a shop on an expressway strip** |
| "We would like to inform you that" | "We will tell you" | 11 words → 4 words, and it sounds like a person |
| "Should you require assistance" | "If you need help" | Same |
| "Sorry for the inconvenience" | "Thanks for waiting" | 🔴 "Sorry for the inconvenience" reads as a corporate apology for something that was our fault. Own it instead |
| "Our team of highly trained technicians" | "The mechanic" | The brief's core insight: **308 followers means named people, not corporate scale.** `AGENT-BRIEF.md` §1 |

### "We." First person, active

✅ "We measure it in millimetres." · "We stop and ask first." · "We do not do motorcycles —
we would rather say that clearly than take the job and hand it on."

### Two special cases

| Case | What to do |
|---|---|
| **When we are at fault** | Say so **first**, in the first sentence, with no preamble. 🔴 "Sorry for the inconvenience" is the thing to avoid — it hides the fault behind a formality. A short, direct admission is worth more than any apology |
| **When we cannot help** | 🔑 **Say "no" clearly, then say what we do cover.** "No — we're cars and light-commercials only." That is a good answer, and it is the most credible sentence on the site. `content/copy-notes.md` phrase 8 |

## 5. Taglish boundaries

**Taglish is a tool, not a costume.** Used well it is the mark of a shop that speaks the
customer's language. Used badly it is a website that tried too hard.

### 5.1 Where Taglish is welcome

| Place | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **FAQ question headings** | "Bakit umaaano ang gulay ko?" | 🔑 **This is a real query someone types into Google.** Writing it verbatim is both better SEO and better service. `seo-keywords.md` Cluster M |
| **FAQ answers, for local vocabulary** | "**Naka-tulala na ang bolts ng preno** — that is a warped brake disc" | The mechanic's own phrase, glossed. The customer recognises their own description and stops translating in their head |
| **A first line of an important answer** | "Ang tibok ay tumutulong din sa lahat ng brakes." | Warm, then switch to English. **One Taglish line per answer, not a paragraph** |
| **Service micro-copy at a pinch point** | "Wala nang bayan. I-book mo na." | Only where it is unmistakably conversational and the audience is mobile and local |

### 5.2 Where Taglish is banned

| Place | Why |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **The privacy notice and terms** | 🔴 **Long legal prose in Taglish is a compliance failure.** A consent notice the reader does not fully understand **is not informed consent.** Plain, clear English only. `legal-compliance-ph.md` §7, `risks-dos-donts.md` AP29 |
| 🔴 **Prices, durations, and any number** | "₱1,200 hanggang ₱2,500" is harder to parse, not easier. **Numbers stay in clean English format** |
| 🔴 **Headings and `<h1>`/`<h2>`** | Mixing scripts in a heading looks like an error at small sizes, and it fragments the SEO signal |
| **The About page's factual claims** | Hours, address, brand claims — English, and verifiable |
| **Buttons and CTAs** | "Book a bay" · "Call now" · "Get directions". English, and short |
| **Anything a screen reader must pronounce** | Taglish is genuinely hard to read aloud. **WCAG 2.2** |
| **Error messages** | 🔴 "Hindi natatanggap" on a failed booking is worse than "That did not go through. Please try again." — the reader needs to understand the problem, not enjoy the phrasing |

### 5.3 The mechanical rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Keep the sentence structure English; borrow the noun** | ✅ "Your brakes are **naka-tulala na**" is wrong. ✅ "**Naka-tulala na ang bolts ng preno** — that is a warped disc" is right. **Taglish lives in the noun phrase, not the grammar** |
| **Gloss on first use, then English** | Taglish term → brief English explanation → English from then on. The reader learns the shop's word once and does not have to keep decoding |
| **Never translate a Filipino word into an English one that sounds superior** | "Tulala" is not "warped" in the same way, and a Balanga mechanic will hear the substitution. **Use the mechanic's word and explain it** |
| **No machine translation** | 🔴 Machine-translated Tagalog is immediately recognisable, and in this market it reads as a business that does not speak the language. **If you are unsure of a phrase, ask — do not translate** |
| **No "Filipino-style" English** | Dropping inflections, or writing "we will implements", is not Taglish. It is an error. **Fix the grammar** |

## 6. How to write prices honestly

**The most important section in this file.** Publishing a price is a **legal
representation** (`legal-compliance-ph.md` §4.3), and the price page is also the
differentiator (`competitors.md` §3). Both facts must be respected at once.

### 6.1 The five rules

| # | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **A range's low end must be genuinely payable** | If EYG's basic PMS is ₱2,000, the range is **₱2,000–2,500**. It is not "from ₱1,200". 🚫 **Publishing a floor you will not honour is bait-and-switch, regardless of the small print** |
| **P2** | **Name the variable that moves the price** | 🔑 "**₱1,200–2,500 depending on oil type and vehicle size.**" — the customer can self-select and stop worrying. A bare range makes them assume the top. `risks-dos-donts.md` AP25 |
| **P3** | **Say the grade out loud** | "A basic PMS is ₱1,200–2,500 with **conventional oil**. Synthetic is ₱2,500–5,500." — 🔴 **The single most common complaint in the PH auto trade is not a bad shop; it is not being told the oil was synthetic.** Killing that is free |
| **P4** | **Always carry the qualifier** | "Indicative. **We confirm the price in writing before we start.**" Two words of copy that converts a legal exposure into a trust signal |
| **P5** | **Never invent a price to fill a gap** | Some services have no honest range. `pricing: CALL_FOR_PRICE` and a plain "Call us for a quote on this one" is **more honest and probably converts better**, because it is the same answer the customer gets from every good shop, and the next line — "we will tell you before we start" — is what actually wins them |

### 6.2 Worked examples

**✅ Right — PMS**
> **PMS — Preventive Maintenance Service**
> Oil, oil filter, a 40-point inspection, and a **written report** of what we find.
>
> **₱1,200 – ₱2,500** with conventional oil.
> **₱2,500 – ₱5,500** with synthetic oil.
>
> *The oil grade is the biggest difference, and it is worth being straight about which one
> your car needs. We will tell you before we start.*
>
> **2–3 hours.** Anything we find gets quoted and approved by you first — we do not just
> get on with it.

**✅ Right — tyres**
> **Tyres + fitting**
> When you ask us for a tyre, we quote the **complete installed price in one number** —
> the tyre, the mounting, the balancing, and the valve. Not the tyre on its own, because
> that is not what you will pay.
>
> *Sizes, brands and prices change, so call us and we will tell you what we have in stock
> today. That is faster than a price list that is out of date by next week.*

**✅ Right — the thing we do not do**
> **Do you do undercoating?**
> Not yet. What we do have is **underchassis maintenance** — cleaning, inspecting, rust
> treatment and repair underneath, with photos of what we find.
>
> If undercoating is what you need, we would rather tell you now than take the car in for
> something we do not do.

**❌ Wrong**
> ~~PMS from ₱899!! Special promo this week only. Book now and get 20% off!~~

Three separate violations: a floor we cannot honour, a fabricated urgency, and a discount
with no terms. `risks-dos-donts.md` N2, N3, AP24

### 6.3 Prices in bundles

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Print the à-la-carte sum next to the bundle price** | 🔑 **₱750 — normally ₱1,550.** One line, and it makes the saving self-evidencing. It is also the clearest possible expression of the strategy: nobody else will print their own maths |
| **The saving is real arithmetic** | Never inflate it. If the à-la-carte sum is a range, show the range, or price the bundle against the **low** end and say so |
| **"What's not included" is on the same card, same weight** | 🔴 The bundle card's most important line. Small grey text on a bundle is a bait-and-switch template |
| **No crossed-out prices, no "was/now"** | 🚫 A struck-through price implies a reference price that was actually charged. Without evidence of that, it is a fabricated representation |

### 6.4 The four sentences that make pricing trustworthy

> **"We will confirm the price in writing before we start."**
> **"We stop and ask first."**
> **"We never add work you did not approve."**
> **"Your old tyre comes back to you."**

**These cost nothing, they are entirely true of an honest shop, and together they are worth
more than every discount EYG could invent** — because **no competitor in Balanga can say
them.** Every one of them is the exact thing a customer in this market has been burned by.

## 7. Mechanics and formatting

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Short sentences.** | `AGENT-BRIEF.md` §5. **One idea per sentence.** Most: ~20 words |
| **Active voice** | "We measure", not "measurement is taken" |
| **Second person** | "You", not "one" or "the customer" |
| **Sentence case in headings** | "What we do not do" beats "What We Do Not Do". 🔑 **EXCEPT** service names, which stay **Title Case** because that is the shop's own vocabulary and the SEO term. `facebook-intel.md` §2.3 |
| **No exclamation marks in body copy** | One per CTA maximum |
| **Numbers: use digits** | "8 AM", "5 PM", "40-point inspection", "1.6 mm". 🔴 "1.6 mm" is a **measurement** — precision is the point |
| **Philippines format** | ₱1,200 · ₱4,500 · 8:00 AM · 8 AM · Monday to Saturday (no "Mon–Sat" in prose; the abbreviation belongs in a schema block) |
| **No emoji in website body copy** | §3. Emoji belong in the FAQ, and in social |
| **No "click here"** | Name the destination: "See the full service list", "Get directions to EGSA Fourlanes" |
| **Link text that survives being read aloud** | ✅ "Book a bay" · ✅ "Get directions" · ✅ "Read what we collect and why" |
| **Alt text describes the work, never the person** | ✅ "Brake pads and disc rotor on the front axle of a silver sedan" · ❌ "Customer Juan with his new tyres". `legal-compliance-ph.md` §2.6 |
| **Buttons: verb first** | "Book a bay" · "Call now" · "Get directions" · "See the price list" — not "Submit", "Click", "Learn more" |
| **Read it aloud** | 🔑 **The single best test.** If you stumble, the customer will too. It is on a phone, in traffic |

## 8. Two short templates that carry the strategy

### Homepage hero

> # Tyres, PMS, alignment and brakes — on EGSA Fourlanes, Balanga City
>
> **Open Monday to Saturday, 8 AM to 5 PM.** We will tell you the price before we start,
> and we never add work you did not approve.
>
> **[ Call now ]** · **[ Book a bay ]**
>
> A listed Michelin and BFGoodrich dealer. Written inspection report with every PMS.

**Why this works:** the hours are verified and nobody else publishes them; the price
promise is the positioning in nine words; the two buttons are the only two things a
stranded or hurried driver needs; the last line is checkable proof and a real artefact.

### The honest "no" — the highest-value block on the site

> ## Things we don't do
>
> We are a car and light-commercial shop. So we will tell you up front:
>
> - **Motorcycles** — cars and light-commercials only
> - **Roadside or after-hours** — we are not a 24-hour service and we will not pretend to be
> - **Bodywork and painting** — not in-house
> - **Engine rebuilds** — we diagnose, we do not rebuild
>
> If you need one of these, we will tell you who to ask. And if it is something we do, we
> will say so before you drive over.

> 🔑 **This block is the strongest asset on the website and it is worth more than any
> discount.** It is the only part of a local auto site that a competitor cannot copy
> without changing how they actually run their business — and it is exactly the opposite of
> what every one of the 138 businesses in this city is doing. `competitors.md` §3

## 9. The pre-publish check

**Five questions. Any "no" means it does not ship.** `ux-benchmarks-auto-shop.md` §6.4

| # | Question | If no |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Could the shop's own receptionist defend this claim to a customer who challenged it?** | 🔴 **Do not ship it.** This is the best proxy test, and it catches fabrication, fake urgency and unprovable pricing in one pass |
| 2 | **Have I used a specific part, size, number or time instead of an adjective?** | Rewrite. Concrete beats all |
| 3 | **Is the Taglish in a place a customer would actually speak, and glossed on first use?** | Move it or cut it. §5 |
| 4 | **Is there a price with its variable named and its qualifier attached?** | Fix before publishing. §6 |
| 5 | **Could this be said by all 138 competitors and still be true?** | 🔴 **Then it is worthless.** Replace it with something only EYG can say — a measured number, a real landmark, a checkable brand record, or an honest "no" |

**And the single sentence that decides everything:**

> **Nobody in Balanga publishes their hours, their prices, or a way to book. If EYG's website
> does — and the forecourt keeps the promise — that is the whole business case. If it
> doesn't keep the promise, the website has made things worse, because now there is
> evidence.**
