### 14. How do I find my tyre size? ✅ Answerable now

**Look inside the driver's door jamb.** There is a sticker or plate listing the tyre size,
like `205/55R16`. It is a legal requirement for it to be there.

**How to read it:**
- `205` = width in millimetres
- `55` = aspect ratio — the sidewall height as a percentage of the width
- `R16` = the rim size in inches
- A trailing letter like `H` or `V` is the speed rating

**If the sticker is gone or unreadable, come in and we will read it off your tyres in
two minutes.** Bring your car and we will check while you wait.

### 18. What is nitrogen and is it worth it? ✅ Answerable now

**Nitrogen instead of air in your tyres.** Nitrogen molecules are bigger and leak out
slower, so the pressure holds longer. You check less often, and fuel economy can improve
slightly.

**It is not a gimmick, but it is not essential either.** If you check your pressures
regularly, air is fine.

⚠️ *Owner: confirm nitrogen pricing per tyre and whether it is free with a tyre purchase.
**This is a very common bait-and-switch in this trade** — "free nitrogen" quietly becomes
a per-tyre charge at handover. State the price before the work, on the job order.
`services.md` §1.4*

### 19. How often should I replace my tyres? ✅ Answerable now

**The legal minimum in the Philippines is 1.6 mm.** We measure and give you the number in
millimetres, not a "looks okay".

**As a rule of thumb, most manufacturers say replace at around **1.6–2.0 mm** — not
because that is safe, but because below it you are driving on the carcass.

**Check your tread at least every few months, and definitely before the rains.** Many
people never check once after the tyres go on. That is what our free tyre-pressure check
is for.

# BLOCK D — Alignment, handling and safety

### 22. Bakit umaaano ang gulay ko? *(Why is my car pulling to one side?)* ✅ Answerable now

**"Umaaano" means pulling to one side.** In Filipino roadside language, that is *umaaano*.

**The most common causes, in order:**

1. **Wheel alignment out** — the most common cause by far
2. **One tyre more worn or a different pressure on one side** — check this first, it is free
3. **A worn or damaged tyre** on that side
4. **Brake dragging on one side** — if it pulls worse when you brake
5. **Bent rim or a previous impact**

**Start with the free one:** check all four pressures against the sticker on your door
jamb. If they all match and it still pulls, it is alignment — and we will measure and
show you the numbers before and after.

**Is it safe to drive?** Usually, yes, for a while. But if the steering is heavy, or
vibrating, or the car feels vague, **do not wait — get it checked.** Nobody in this town
is going to check it for free, and a pull you ignore becomes a worn tyre you have to
replace.

---

### 23. My steering wheel is off-centre but the car goes straight. Is that a problem? ✅ Answerable now

**Yes, usually.** "Gstraight pero ang manakidle ay skewed" — the car tracks straight but
the wheel sits off-centre — almost always means the alignment has drifted, often after a
tyre change, a kerb hit, or a pothole.

**Drive on a straight, flat road, hands loose, and look at where the rim sits. If it is
not centred, get the alignment checked.** It is cheap and it protects your tyres from
uneven wear.

**Ask for camber, not just toe.** Their verified service is *"Wheel Alignment and Camber
Correction"* — so EYG measures more than a basic toe adjustment. `facebook-intel.md` §2.3

---

### 24. My brakes are making a noise. Is that normal? ✅ Answerable now

**No. Brakes are meant to be quiet.** Common causes, in order:

1. **Worn brake pads** — a squeal when you brake is the pad indicator doing its job
2. **"Naka-tulala na ang bolts ng preno"** — warped brake disc. A low grinding or pulsing
   feeling, usually worse when you brake firmly. This one gets worse and it can be
   expensive to leave
3. **A stone or debris caught in the caliper**
4. **A sticking caliper or slider pin** — dragging on one side, which will also make the
   car pull

**We measure pad and disc thickness front and rear and write it down.** You get the
numbers, you decide. 🔴 *This is exactly the service where the "should I do the rears too"
upsell happens in this trade. **We report both axles in writing and you decide. We do not
add an axle you did not agree to.** `risks-dos-donts.md` §3.2*

### 28. What should I do before the rainy season? ✅ Answerable now

**A tyre check, a wiper check, and a brake check.** That is genuinely most of it.

**Tyres.** Rain plus standing water plus debris is how a bad tyre becomes a blowout.
Tread depth measured in millimetres, not eyeballed.

**Wipers.** A worn wiper in the rain is nearly as bad as a bald tyre. If they smear or
streak, replace them.

**Brakes.** Wet, and the stopping distance is longer. Worth having the pad and disc
thickness measured before you need it.

**Lights.** Cheap to check, and you will not notice a dead one until you need it.

**And this one most people skip:** if you drive out of province — Baguio, Zambales, Subic,
or Cubao — **check the spare and the jack before you leave.** Not the tyre. The spare.

**We have put all of this into one package: the Rainy Season Safety Package.** 🔴 *Owner:
confirm the package is genuinely available. `content/packages.md` Package 1*

> every competitor will say yes to everything, saying *"no, we're cars only"*, *"not at
> night, and we will not pretend otherwise"*, or *"we do not do that — here is what we do
> cover"* is the most credible thing this shop can say. **It is also the cheapest possible
> differentiator, and no competitor can copy it without changing how they actually
> operate.**
