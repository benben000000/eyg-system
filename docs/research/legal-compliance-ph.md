## 1. Business registration and what "trade name" claims are legal

### 1.1 Which DTI/SEC registration applies

There is a live, unresolved question about EYG's own legal form, and it changes the
paperwork. Resolve it first.

| If EYG is… | Registered with | Evidence to file | NPCRS paperwork |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship** | **DTI** — Department Registration + **Business Name (BN) registration** | DTI Certificate of Registration; barangay/mayor's business permit | DTI Certificate of Registration + business permit (per NPCRS FAQ, "Sole Proprietorships") |
| **Corporation** | **SEC** — Certificate of Registration | SEC Certificate of Registration; certified-true-copy latest **General Information Sheet**; Articles of Incorporation if COR unavailable; valid business permit | Notarised **Secretary's Certificate** *explicitly stating APPOINTMENT or DESIGNATION* of the DPO + SEC COR + certified-true-copy GIS + business permit |
| One Person Corporation | SEC | SEC COR + business permit | Notarised Secretary's Certificate or signed-by-director appointment letter + SEC COR + permit |

> - the **legal name must appear on the Terms page, the footer, the privacy notice and

### 1.3 Does an auto shop need DTI accreditation to advertise PMS?

**No. There is no DTI accreditation scheme that an ordinary automotive service shop must
hold in order to advertise or perform PMS.**

What is actually required to *operate*:

- **DTI business-name / department registration** (for a sole proprietorship) or
  **SEC registration** (for a corporation) — a **registration**, not an accreditation.
- A **barangay / city business permit** — a local licence, not a DTI accreditation. The
  NPCRS FAQ explicitly requires a *"valid business permit"* and asks what to file *"in
  replacement of the business or mayor's permit"* if the permit is specific to the
  location and industry (e.g. PEZA `Permit to Operate`, or a freeport registration).
  That is a **local government** requirement, not DTI.
- **BIR registration** (TIN) — a tax matter.
- **LTO / Land Transportation Office** — no mechanic-licensing prerequisite (see §6).

**What PMS advertising itself does NOT require:** there is no DTI, DOST, or government
body that must certify, witness, or approve an advertisement saying *"we do PMS."*

**Consequence for the site:** you may advertise PMS freely. What you may **not** do is
imply a government or brand endorsement that does not exist. So:

| Admissible | Inadmissible |
|---|---|
| "Preventive Maintenance Service (PMS) — Mon to Sat 8AM to 5PM" | "DTI-accredited PMS" |
| "Listed Michelin & BFGoodrich dealer" | "Michelin-authorised service centre" |
| "₱1,200–₱2,500 PMS" *(once the owner signs off the range)* | "The cheapest PMS in Bataan" |
| "OBD scanning & resetting" | "Certified diagnostics" |

## 2. Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) — what it means for a booking form

**Full text verified at `https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act/` (National Privacy
Commission, official), read 2026-10-02.** This is the primary legal constraint on the
booking flow, the lead forms, the gallery, and the SMS confirmations.

### 2.1 EYG *is* a Personal Information Controller (PIC)

RA 10173 **Sec. 3(h)** defines a *personal information controller* as a person or
organisation who controls the collection, holding, processing or use of personal
information. **EYG is that entity** for every name, phone number, email address, vehicle
detail and plate number it collects through this website. Two consequences:

1. **The entire DPA applies to the site**, not just to a hypothetical future enterprise
   feature. A contact form is enough.
2. **Sec. 21(b)** obliges the PIC to **designate an individual accountable for compliance**,
   and *"the identity of the individual(s) so designated shall be made known to any data
   subject **upon request**."* EYG must be able to name that person today. That is
   typically the owner or a senior staff member, and **their name and contact details
   belong on the privacy page**.

### 2.2 What EYG collects, and how each item is classified

`CreateBookingInput` in `src/lib/types.ts` collects: `name`, `phone`, `email?`,
`startAt`, `serviceIds`, `packageId?`, `promoCode?`,
`vehicle { year, make, model, variant?, plate?, mileageKm? }`, `notes?`,
`consentSms`, `consentMarketing?`, plus honeypot and CAPTCHA fields.

| Field | Classification | RA 10173 basis | Handling rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `name` | **Personal information** — Sec. 3(g) | Directly identifies | Consent or contract; retain only as long as needed |
| `phone` | **Personal information** | Directly identifies | Needed to confirm the booking. Also the SMS channel |
| `email` | **Personal information** | Directly identifies | **Make optional.** Collecting an email you never use is itself a proportionality failure |
| `vehicle.year / make / model` | **Personal information** | Identifies the owner via the vehicle in combination with the name | Legitimate interest: you cannot quote accurately without it |
| **`vehicle.plate`** | **Personal information — and arguably SENSITIVE** | **Sec. 3(l)(3):** *"Issued by government agencies peculiar to an individual which includes, but not limited to, social security numbers, previous or current health records, licenses or its denials, suspension or revocation, and tax returns."* A plate is **LTO-issued** and peculiar to an individual. | 🚨 **See §2.3 — treat as sensitive.** |
| `vehicle.mileageKm` | **Personal information** | Links to service history | Legitimate interest for quote accuracy |
| `notes` (free text) | **Personal information — and a leakage risk** | Customers routinely type health, financial and personal information into a free-text box: *"my brakes are making this noise and I also take my BP meds…"* | 🚨 **Never publish. Never surface in the `/admin` board without redaction.** See §2.4 |
| `consentSms`, `consentMarketing` | **Personal information** (a recorded consent is itself PI) | Consent evidence must be retained — Sec. 3(b) requires consent be *"evidenced by written, electronic or recorded means"* | Store the consent record, timestamp it, with the form version |
| Booking history | **Personal information** | Retention is a live obligation — Sec. 11(e)/(f) | See §2.5 |
| Photos containing faces | **Personal information** | Sec. 3(g) | See §2.6 |
| Photos containing plates | **Personal information / arguably sensitive** | Sec. 3(l)(3) | See §2.6 |

### 2.3 🔴 The plate-number question — the most important privacy judgement on this site

**Is a Philippine vehicle plate number *sensitive personal information* under RA 10173
Sec. 3(l)(3)?**

**The honest answer: it is genuinely unsettled, and the conservative reading is "treat it
as sensitive."**

- **The case that it IS sensitive:** Sec. 3(l)(3) covers personal information *"issued by
  government agencies peculiar to an individual"*, and enumerates *"social security
  numbers, previous or current health records, licenses or its denials, suspension or
  revocation, and tax returns."* A plate number is issued by the **LTO**, is peculiar to
  an individual, and is a **licence-plate identifier** — close analogue to "licenses."
  Combined with a name, it is directly identifying, and it is used for identity and
  enforcement purposes.
- **The case that it is not:** the NPC's public guidance I retrieved does not name
  vehicle plates in its enumerations, and the word "licenses" may be read as
  professional licences. A plate is arguably a vehicle identifier, not a personal
  identifier in the intended sense.
- **⚠️ SOURCE NOT RETRIEVED:** I could not obtain the RA 10173 **IRR** (the NPC PDF
  returned 404) or an NPC circular/circular opinion specifically addressing plate numbers.
  **The IRR's sensitive-personal-information rules are the most likely place this is
  resolved, and I have not read them.**

**Therefore, the operational rule for this build — treat plates as sensitive:**

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Do not publish a plate number on any public page** | Not in `/gallery`, not in a testimonial, not in a review, not in a schema field |
| **Do not put a plate in a URL** | `/gallery?plate=ABC-123` is a public, indexable, shareable identifier. Never |
| **Do not put a plate in an unblurred photo** | See §2.6 |
| **Do collect it, if at all** | Only for the bay-order workflow, with explicit consent, and it must be on the `sensitive` consent line — **not** bundled into the general service consent |
| **Do not retain it longer than the job needs it** | Sec. 11(e)/(f) |
| **Do let a data subject have it corrected or deleted** | Sec. 16(d)–(e) |

> **Recommendation to the orchestrator:** the `CreateBookingInput.plate` field in
> `src/lib/types.ts` is `plate?: string` — **optional**. Good. **Keep it optional. Do not
> make it required.** And add a separate, un-pre-ticked consent flag for it if it is
> collected at all.

### 2.4 Lawful basis — Sec. 12 — and the two-consent problem

RA 10173 **Sec. 12** permits processing of personal information only if **at least one**
of six conditions exists. For a booking form, the realistic bases are:

| Basis | Sec. 12 | Applies when |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Consent** | ✅ | The customer ticks the box. This is the primary basis and the only one to rely on for anything optional |
| **(b) Contract** | ✅ | *"necessary and is related to the fulfillment of a contract with the data subject **or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject prior to entering into a contract**"* — **this squarely covers taking the booking details in order to service the car.** This is the strongest basis for name + phone + vehicle + slot |
| **(c) Legal obligation** | ✅ | BIR record-keeping; receipt issuance |
| (d) Vital interests | ⚠️ Only if the data subject is physically unable to consent | Do **not** rely on this for marketing or convenience |
| (e) Public order / public authority | ❌ Not for a private shop | Do not misuse |
| **(f) Legitimate interests** | ⚠️ Available, *"except where such interests are overridden by fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject"* | Marketing by SMS is **not** legitimate interest — use consent |

**🔴 CRITICAL — the two-consent problem.** `CreateBookingInput` has
`consentSms: boolean` and `consentMarketing?: boolean`. These are **different
permissions** and must not be conflated:

- **`consentSms`** — permission to send the **transactional** SMS: *"Your booking EYG-XXXX
  is confirmed for Tue 7 Oct, 8:30 AM."* This is arguably covered by the Sec. 12(b)
  contract basis and is essential to the service. It must be **presented as necessary**,
  pre-ticked or clearly required, and it must be obvious that declining it means no
  confirmation SMS.
- **`consentMarketing`** — permission to send **promotional** SMS: *"Tyre clearance this
  weekend!"* This is **direct marketing** (Sec. 3(d)) and needs **its own, separate,
  unticked, specific consent.** It is not required to book. It is opt-in.

**The design rule, and it is non-negotiable:**

> **One action, two separately-labelled permissions, and it must be visually obvious
> which is which.** Do not use a single "I agree" checkbox covering both. Do not
> pre-tick either. Do not bundle marketing consent into the booking submit. Do not
> dark-pattern it: no *"Decline" is hidden in smaller grey text than "Accept."*

### 2.5 Retention — Sec. 11(e) and (f)

> *"Retained only for as long as necessary for the fulfillment of the purposes for which
> the data was obtained **or for the establishment, exercise or defense of legal claims, or
> for legitimate business purposes, or as provided by law**"*

> *"Kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects **for no longer than is
> necessary**"*

**⚠️ SOURCE NOT RETRIEVED:** the **IRR's** Rule on Retention sets a baseline of **five
(5) years** for personal data generally, and much longer for some categories. **I could
not retrieve the IRR and am not asserting the 5-year figure as verified.** The NPC's
registration FAQ also refers to a *"Rule on Retention"*; that is the document to read.

**Recommended retention schedule for EYG** — marked as a **policy proposal requiring
owner sign-off**, not a legal conclusion:

| Data | Keep for | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Completed job record: name, phone, vehicle, service, price, **date** | 🔴 **5 years from the job** *(pending IRR verification)* | Tax records, warranty defence, repeat-customer service history. This is the core business record |
| Booking in progress (not yet serviced) | Until the job completes + 90 days | No basis to hold it longer |
| **Plate number** | **The job only.** Delete at handover. | Proportionality — Sec. 11(d) *"adequate and not excessive"* |
| Free-text `notes` | The job only. Redact from the admin board. | Data-minimisation |
| **SMS consent record** (timestamped, with the consent text version) | 🔴 **As long as the marketing relationship + the period needed to prove consent** | Consent evidence is itself PI. If you cannot prove consent, you cannot show you had it — Sec. 3(b) |
| Contact-form leads that never became jobs | **12 months**, then delete | Reasonable business purpose expires |
| **Analytics / ad identifiers** | See §6 | |
| Photos with identifiable people or plates | See §2.6 | |

**Deletion must be real, not aspirational.** RA 10173 **Sec. 27** penalises *"improper
disposal"* — knowing or negligent disposal of personal information in a publicly
accessible area, or leaving it in a container for trash collection — with **6 months to 2
years' imprisonment and ₱100,000–₱500,000** for personal information, and **1–3 years and
₱100,000–₱1,000,000** for sensitive personal information.

> **Operational consequence:** the backups that will eventually be deleted are the real
> risk. **A retention policy that is not enforced in the database backup rotation is not a
> retention policy.** Ask `backend-core`/`devops` how backups expire.

### 2.6 🚨 Photos — faces, plates, and before/after galleries

`/gallery` is on the locked page map, and I have extracted 15 real job photos from the
shop's own Facebook posts (`facebook-intel.md` §2.11.3). They show completed work on real
customer vehicles. **Publishing them is not free.** Read this before a single image ships.

**The three legal exposures:**

1. **Plates.** LTO-issued, individual-specific, arguably sensitive (§2.3). A before/after
   gallery with unblurred plates is a searchable public index of who drives what.
2. **Faces.** Personal information under Sec. 3(g). The shop is the PIC of these images
   whether or not the photographer is.
3. **A photo is a statement about a person.** *"A Toyota Hilux FX on our alignment rack"*
   is fine. *"Mr. Dela Cruz's Hilux, now fixed"* is a claim about a named individual,
   published, indexed, and permanent.

**The required protocol — all four, before any job photo goes live:**

| # | Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Blur or crop every plate** | Blur the plate region, or crop below the bumper line. **Verify every single image at every breakpoint** — a crop that hides the plate on mobile may not on desktop |
| 2 | **Faces: blur, or get a release** | A written, signed, specific, **revocable** release naming the site, the images and the period. Keep it on file. Absent a release, blur. **Do not assume a Facebook post implies website consent** — posting publicly is not the same as authorising commercial advertising |
| 3 | **No customer names** | Never, anywhere, next to a photo. Name + photo + car model is a dossier |
| 4 | **Alt text describes the work, not the person** | ✅ `"Brake pads and disc rotor on the front axle of a silver sedan"` · ❌ `"Customer Juan with his new tyres"` |

**Also, and this is free legal cover:** the shop's own Facebook post template already does
the right thing — it names the **vehicle**, never the customer:

> "🚘: Toyota Hilux FX … Replaced Steering Rack Assembly / Wheel Alignment"

**Follow the shop's own discipline.** Add one line of caption, e.g.
*"Photo of our work on a customer's Toyota Hilux FX. Customer name withheld with consent."*
That single sentence is a meaningful reduction in risk and it costs nothing.

**A lower-risk alternative worth considering:** photograph the **bay, the tools, the
alignment rack, the diagnostic scanner, the parts on the bench** — your own property,
your own equipment, zero third-party rights — and pair it with job photos that are
heavily cropped to the work area. A gallery of *"the job, not the customer"* is both
safer and, honestly, more useful to a prospective buyer.

### 2.7 Rights — Sec. 16 — and what you must be able to do today

RA 10173 **Sec. 16** entitles every data subject to: (a) be informed; (b) be **furnished
the information in Sec. 16(b) *before* their personal information enters the system**;
(c) reasonable access on demand; (d) to **dispute inaccuracy and have it corrected
immediately**; (e) to **suspend, withdraw, block, remove or destroy**; (f) to be
**indemnified for damages** from inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, false, unlawfully
obtained or unauthorised use.

**Sec. 16(b) requires the privacy notice to state, before collection:**

> (1) a description of the personal information to be entered; (2) the purposes; (3) the
> **scope and method** of processing; (4) the recipients or classes of recipients;
> (5) methods of **automated access**, if allowed, and the extent authorised; (6) the
> **identity and contact details of the PIC or its representative**; (7) the **period for
> which the information will be stored**; and (8) the existence of their rights — to
> access, to correct, and to lodge a complaint before the Commission.

**→ This is the exact structure required for the consent copy in §7. Map each subsection
to a paragraph.**

**Sec. 16(f) indemnity is the under-rated one.** A customer whose phone number is sold to
a spam list, or whose plate number appears in a public gallery and is used to track the
vehicle, has a statutory indemnity claim. That is a **direct financial exposure to a
business with a 308-follower customer base.**

**Operating requirements this creates — all need a real owner, not an auto-reply:**

- [ ] A **named DPO** whose identity and contact details are published and given on request (Sec. 21(b))
- [ ] A **process** to answer a Sec. 16(c) access request — the booking database must be
      queryable by phone number and by name
- [ ] A **process** to correct a wrong record (Sec. 16(d)) — *immediately*, and Sec. 16(d)
      requires notifying third parties who previously received the inaccurate data
- [ ] A **process** to delete on request (Sec. 16(e)), including from backups
- [ ] A **complaint route** to the NPC on the privacy page and in the footer
- [ ] A **breach-response plan** — see §2.8

### 2.8 🔴 Breach notification — Sec. 20(f)

> The PIC *"shall promptly notify the Commission and affected data subjects when
> **sensitive personal information** or other information that may, under the
> circumstances, be used to **enable identity fraud** are reasonably believed to have
> been acquired by an unauthorized person, and the PIC or the Commission believes that
> such unauthorized acquisition is likely to give rise to a **real risk of serious harm**."*

The notification must **at least** describe the nature of the breach, the sensitive
personal information possibly involved, and the measures taken. Delay is permitted only to
the extent necessary to determine scope, prevent further disclosure, or restore integrity.

**Practical triggers for a booking site of this size:**

| Incident | Notify? |
|---|---|
| A staff member exports the booking list and shares it outside the shop | 🔴 **Yes** — this is identity-enabling data |
| A public `/admin` bookings board is left unauthenticated or is indexed by Google | 🔴 **Yes, likely.** Customer names + phones + plates = a ready-made identity-fraud dataset |
| A misconfigured third-party (analytics, chat widget, form service) receives the whole payload | 🔴 **Yes** |
| A lost laptop with a local copy of the customer database | 🔴 **Yes** |
| An incorrect record was sent to the wrong customer | ⚠️ Possibly — Sec. 16(d) correction duty; assess against the "real risk of serious harm" test |
| A failed spam attempt, no data obtained | ❌ Not required |

> **🔴 HARD ENGINEERING REQUIREMENT — from `risks-dos-donts.md` §2 item 2.**
> **`/admin` must be auth-gated, must be `Disallow:` in `robots.txt`, and must carry
> `noindex`.** A publicly-indexable customer booking list containing names, phone
> numbers and vehicle plates is simultaneously a **Data Privacy Act breach**, a
> **Data Privacy Act negligence offence under Sec. 26** (1–3 years, ₱500,000–₱2,000,000),
> and the most expensive possible first impression. This is the single highest-severity
> technical risk in the build. Route it: `backend-core` owns `/admin` API auth,
> `devops` owns the header, `qa` owns the test.

### 2.9 Sub-processors — Sec. 14 and Sec. 21(a)

> **Sec. 14:** *"A personal information controller may subcontract the processing of
> personal information: Provided, That the personal information controller shall be
> responsible for ensuring that proper safeguards are in place…"*
> **Sec. 21(a):** the PIC *"shall use **contractual or other reasonable means** to provide
> a **comparable level of protection** while the information are being processed by a
> third party."*

**Every third party that touches customer data is a sub-processor, and EYG is fully
accountable for it.** For this build that is at minimum:

| Sub-processor | Data it sees | Contractual control needed |
|---|---|---|
| SMS gateway (for booking confirmations) | phone number, name, booking ref | DPA; purpose limitation; no onward marketing; retention |
| Email delivery | email, name | Same |
| Database / hosting provider | full booking record | Same; verify the data-centre region |
| Analytics | page views; **IP address** | 🔴 **IP address is personal information.** Must be disclosed, must respect the retention schedule, must not enable cross-site tracking of a customer without consent |
| Booking CAPTCHA / anti-bot service | IP, possibly behaviour data | Same |
| Map embed (Google/Apple) | **IP address**, and referrer may carry the page you were on | Disclose. Consider a privacy-preserving map link for users who decline cookies |
| Payment (if credit instalment is ever offered) | 🔴 **Financial data** | **Highest risk sub-processor.** If EYG ever takes card details, this needs a proper assessment and almost certainly a PCI-DSS-scoped provider, not a hand-rolled form |

**The `/privacy` page must name the sub-processors.** A generic "we may share data with
partners" is not a Sec. 16(b)(4) disclosure of *"the recipients or classes of
recipients."*

## 3. SMS and promotional messaging

### 3.1 The statutory position

RA 10173 **Sec. 3(d)** defines **"direct marketing"** as *"communication by whatever means
of any advertising or marketing material which is directed to particular individuals."*
Promotional SMS to a named mobile number is direct marketing, full stop.

**Sec. 12(f)** permits processing for *"the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by
the personal information controller or by a third party or parties to whom the data is
disclosed, **except where such interests are overridden by fundamental rights and
freedoms of the data subject**."*

**Conclusion, and it is a firm one:** a customer's phone number, submitted in order to get
their car serviced, **does not carry with it consent to be marketed to.** Selling that
number, or adding it to a broadcast list, is:

- processing for a **purpose not declared before collection** — a direct breach of
  **Sec. 11(a)** (*"Collected for specified and legitimate purposes determined and
  declared before, or as soon as reasonably practicable after collection, and later
  processed in a way compatible with such declared, specified and legitimate purposes
  **only**"*), and
- processing for an **unauthorised purpose**, penalised by **Sec. 28** at **1.5 to 5 years'
  imprisonment and ₱500,000 to ₱1,000,000**.

> **⚠️ SOURCE NOT RETRIEVED:** the **NPC circular/advisory on direct marketing and
> unsolicited promotional messaging**. I could not locate the specific issuance. It exists
> — the NPC's site has an *Advisories & Circulars* section — and **it is the one document
> that most precisely states the rules for marketing SMS.** **Owner action: obtain it and
> read it before sending a single promotional SMS.**

### 3.2 The rules this build must follow

| Rule | Implementation |
|---|---|
| **Never** send promotional SMS to a number that has not given marketing consent | `consentMarketing` must be a **separate, unticked, optional** flag. Its absence may never block a booking |
| **Transactional ≠ promotional** | "Your booking is confirmed" = transactional. "Tyre clearance this weekend" = promotional. Never blur them in the same message thread |
| **Record the consent** | Sec. 3(b): consent must be *"evidenced by written, electronic or recorded means."* Store: timestamp, the exact consent text shown, the form version, the source. Without the record you cannot prove you had consent |
| **Every message must identify the sender and offer an opt-out** | Standard requirement; and a practical one in the PH market where spam complaints are the norm |
| **Opt-out must actually work, and be honoured promptly** | An ignored STOP is a continuing breach. Build the suppression list as a real feature, not a manual task. `backend-integrations` owns this |
| **Never pre-tick marketing consent** | And never style the decline option to be less prominent than accept. That is a dark pattern (`risks-dos-donts.md` §2) *and* it undermines the validity of the consent, so it fails twice |
| **Reasonable hours only** | Sending a promo at 22:00 is the kind of complaint that generates an NPC enquiry. 9:00–20:00, and respect the recipient's stated preference |
| **Never repurpose the booking list** | Sec. 11(a): collected for servicing, used for servicing. A separate marketing list, built only from explicit opt-ins, is the only lawful route |
| **Do not buy or scrape numbers** | No lawful basis at all, and it is the origin of most NPC complaints against small PH businesses |

### 3.3 A recommended message discipline

```
TRANSACTIONAL (basis: contract, Sec. 12(b)) — no marketing consent needed
  "EYG: Booking EYG-4821 confirmed. Tue 7 Oct, 8:30 AM, EGSA Fourlanes, Tuyo.
   Change oil + PMS. Reply STOP to opt out of non-essential messages."

PROMOTIONAL (basis: consent, Sec. 12(a)) — consentMarketing = true, and only then
  "EYG: Rainy season tyre check — 15 mins, free pressure + tread check.
   Mon-Sat 8AM-5PM, EGSA Fourlanes Balanga. Reply STOP to opt out."
```

Both carry the sender identity. Both carry the opt-out. Only the second requires consent.
**Never send the second to a number that has not opted in.**

is stable across consumer-protection regimes and which any Philippine lawyer will

> powers.** Priority sections to locate: those on **liability for services**, **product/

> `https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/` or `https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/` from a
> browser that is not blocked).

### 4.2 The risk structure that matters for a service shop

| Risk | What it means for EYG | Mitigation that must exist in the product |
|---|---|---|
| **Conformity with express warranty** | If the site says *"30-day workmanship guarantee"*, EYG has made an **express warranty** and must honour it. | 🚫 `trust.workmanshipGuaranteeDays: 30` in `site.ts` is **fabricated and unagreed.** Publish a warranty only once the owner signs one in writing, with a stated scope and a stated process |
| **Misleading price representations** | Advertising a price or price range, then charging more without disclosure, is the classic deceptive-practice shape. **This is the #1 legal risk created by the `/services` pricing page.** | Every published price/range must be tied to a **written job order the customer signs before work starts**, and the range must be genuinely achievable. See §4.3 |
| **Hidden fees / surprise charges** | Taxes, disposal fees, "shop supplies", "computer diagnostic fee" added at handover without prior disclosure. | 🚨 **Disclose every charge in the job order *before* work begins.** A tier-2 mechanic and a diagnostic fee must be visible on the estimate, not discovered at pickup. This is the single most common complaint in the Philippine auto-shop industry |
| **Bait-and-switch on tyres** | Advertise a tyre at price X, then the vehicle "needs" balancing, alignment, a valve, a repair — until the real cost is 3× X. | See §4.4. This is the industry's defining sin and the reason tyre advertising is distrusted |
| **Warranty on parts** | A battery, tyre or part has the **manufacturer's** warranty, not EYG's, and EYG must not blur the two | State the manufacturer warranty separately from any EYG workmanship commitment. **Never invent a parts warranty** |
| **"No return" on installed parts** | Refusing a remedy for defective work is a much bigger problem than refusing a refund on a shelf item | A clear, written, reasonable rework policy. Publish it |
| **The receipt itself** | See §5 | A receipt is evidence in any dispute, for either side |

### 4.3 🔴 The pricing page — the legal design rule

`content/services.md` publishes **ranges** marked `SUGGESTED — REQUIRES OWNER
CONFIRMATION`. Once the owner signs off, the site's pricing becomes a **representation**.
Three non-negotiables:

1. **A range must be a range, and the low end must be genuinely payable.** Publishing
   `from ₱1,200` and then quoting ₱1,950 for a "basic" PMS is bait-and-switch. If the
   bottom of the range is a floor, the job that costs ₱1,200 must exist and be available.
2. **A range needs a stated condition.** *"₱1,200–₱2,500 depending on oil type and
   vehicle"* is honest. A bare range invites the argument that the customer was misled
   about *which* end applies. State the variable that moves the price.
3. **Every estimate is confirmed in writing before work starts, and the customer signs
   the job order.** This is not a legal formality — it is the artefact that converts a
   dispute into a settled conversation. **The `/book` flow should be able to produce a
   job-order reference the customer has acknowledged.** `ServiceDto.estimateMin` /
   `estimateMax` and `BookingDto.reference` in `src/lib/types.ts` are the right hooks;
   the **acknowledgement step** is the missing piece and should be raised with the
   orchestrator.

**Also required on the pricing page, and it is a genuine differentiator, not a
compliance chore:** an explicit **"what's not included"** line per service. `content/
services.md` is already structured for this. Publishing exclusions is both good practice
and the strongest possible answer to a bait-and-switch accusation.

### 4.4 🔴 Bait-and-switch on tyres — the specific protocol

This is the industry's defining failure mode and the brief explicitly calls it out. EYG's
own stock posts (Radar 265/65R17, Blacklion 11R22.5, Deestone 185/65-14) show it sells
individual tyres out of stock, which is exactly the transaction where bait-and-switch
happens.

**Rules for the shop, which the website's copy should reinforce:**

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Quote the **complete installed price** — tyre + mounting + balancing + valve, in one number — whenever a customer asks for a tyre | Removes the entire opportunity for the bait-and-switch accusation. A shopper comparing "₱4,500" from one shop and ₱7,800 from another is comparing like with like |
| If mounting/balancing is extra, say so **in the same breath** | "₱4,500 the tyre, plus ₱400 fitting, total ₱4,900" |
| Never add a repair to a tyre sale without the customer saying yes first | If the tyre is damaged beyond use, **say so and stop.** Do not proceed |
| Never change the price after the customer has agreed to it | Any increase requires a new, explicit agreement |
| The old tyre goes back to the customer, or disposal is agreed and priced in advance | Silent disposal of a customer's property is its own complaint generator |
| Show the customer the old tyre | Standard practice in the industry. It is also the best trust device available and costs nothing |

**Website copy that makes this defensible:** a `/services` line reading *"Tyre price
includes mounting and balancing. If we find damage, we stop and ask first — we never add
work you did not approve."* That sentence costs nothing, is entirely true of an honest
shop, and converts the industry's worst reputation into a reason to choose EYG.

## 5. What is legally required on a receipt / invoice

> **⚠️ SOURCE NOT RETRIEVED for the specific BIR revenue-receipt form and the
> documentary-stamp requirements.** The list below is the **practical minimum** any
> Philippine retail/service business must be able to produce. **Owner action #3: confirm
> the exact BIR form, the current documentary-stamp treatment for service receipts, and
> whether EYG (sole prop vs. corporation) has additional registration obligations.
> Start at `https://www.bir.gov.ph/`.**

**What every EYG job receipt must contain:**

| # | Element | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seller's name** — the registered name, exactly | If incorporated: the **corporate name**, not just the trade name |
| 2 | **Business address** | Use the **primary** address consistently. If there are two locations, show **which location performed the work** |
| 3 | **TIN** | Sole prop (individual) or corporate TIN |
| 4 | **BIR business permit / registration details** where applicable | Confirms the business is registered |
| 5 | **Date of service and date of issue** | Two different dates. They diverge constantly and disputes turn on them |
| 6 | **Customer name** | At minimum, surname. Full name is better and is what the booking record holds |
| 7 | **Vehicle description** — make, model, plate | The plate is the link between the receipt and the job. **This is why the receipt is the right place for a plate and the website is not** |
| 8 | **Odometer reading in and out** | 🔴 **The single most useful line on the whole document.** It is the shop's own evidence that nothing extra happened while the car was with them. It is standard practice and almost no small shop does it |
| 9 | **Itemised labour**, each with hours and a rate | *"Brake cleaning — 1.5 h @ ₱400"* beats *"Brake service ₱600"* |
| 10 | **Itemised parts**, each with description, quantity and unit price | **Part brand and model number.** Never just "parts" |
| 11 | **Oil specification and volume**, if oil was used | Grade (e.g. 0W-20 / 5W-40) and litres. A customer who later has an engine fault can otherwise blame the wrong oil |
| 12 | **Subtotal, any taxes, and total in Philippine pesos** | If any tax is collected or passed through, show it as a separate line |
| 13 | **The job-order reference** — matching the website booking | Links the physical job to the digital record. Also makes a disputed charge trivially checkable |
| 14 | **Any warranty** — its scope, its period, and **who provides it** | 🔴 **Distinguish clearly: EYG workmanship warranty vs. manufacturer parts warranty.** Never blur them. If there is no warranty, **say there is none** — silence is not a warranty and an implied one is a legal exposure |
| 15 | **Signature or acknowledged receipt** — customer and EYG | Physical or digital. This is the evidentiary anchor |
| 16 | **Any disposal or shop-supplies fee, itemised** | 🔴 The most common surprise-charge dispute. If it is on the receipt and was on the estimate, it is not a surprise |

**Two design recommendations to pass to the owner and `backend-core`:**

1. **Print a job order (estimate) *before* work and a final invoice *after*, with the same
   reference number.** This is the single most effective consumer-protection practice a
   small shop can adopt, and it costs one sheet of paper.
2. **Record the odometer in and out.** It protects the customer from an unjustified
   repair claim *and* the shop from a theft allegation. It is the highest-value ₱0 addition
   available to this business.

## 6. 🔴 The mechanic-licensing reality — being accurate

**This section exists because it is easy to get wrong, and getting it wrong is a legal
exposure.**

### 6.1 What is actually true

**There is no general national licensing or certification scheme that an ordinary
automotive service technician in the Philippines must hold in order to work in a car
shop.** An automotive service technician is not a profession governed by a licensing
statute in the way that nursing, medicine, law or accountancy are.

**What DOES exist and DOES apply:**

| Body | Reality | Effect on EYG |
|---|---|---|
| **PRC (Professional Regulation Commission)** | Licenses the professions listed in the PRC Law (RA 8981) and Republic Acts. **Automotive service technicians are not among them.** `prc.gov.ph` was unreachable from this environment (`⚠️ TRANSPORT ERROR`) and I have not independently verified the current profession list. | **No PRC licence requirement for EYG's mechanics.** Do **not** claim one. Do **not** claim EYG is "PRC-accredited" |
| **TESDA (TESDA Act, RA 3969)** | **This is the real, correct, and citable credential in the Philippines.** TESDA runs **TVET automotive / mechanical vocational qualifications** — e.g. *Automotive Mechanic*, *Automotive Service Technician* — through **TESDA Training Institutions (TTIs)**. A **TESDA Certificate of Competency** or **TESDA Certificate of Completion** is a genuine, verifiable, government-issued credential. | ✅ **The correct phrasing is: "our mechanic is TESDA-certified in Automotive Servicing."** Name the qualification and the issuing body. That is verifiable and it is exactly the claim a discerning customer respects |
| **Brand training** | Tyre and battery manufacturers run their own technician training and certification (Michelin, BFGoodrich, Amaron, PETRONAS and others all do). | ✅ **If EYG has ever had a technician certified by a brand, that is the strongest credential available and it is brand-verifiable.** Ask the owner. `UNVERIFIED` |
| **LTO** | Licences drivers and registers vehicles. **No mechanic-licensing role.** | None |
| **DTI** | Business registration, not practitioner accreditation. See §1.3 | None |
| **LGU business permit** | Operating licence for the premises | Required, local |

### 6.2 🚫 Claims that are false, and how to spot them

| ❌ False claim | Why it is false | ✅ Defensible replacement |
|---|---|---|
| *"Licensed mechanics"* | No licensing scheme exists to license against | *"Our mechanic holds a TESDA qualification in Automotive Servicing"* — **only if true and named** |
| *"Certified technicians"* | Certified by whom? There is no default certifier | *"TESDA-certified in Automotive Servicing (Automotive Mechanic, 2019)"* — with the year |
| *"Government-licensed auto centre"* | There is no such scheme | *"Registered business with a valid Balanga City business permit"* |
| *"PRC-registered"* | Not a registered profession | Do not use |
| *"DTI-accredited service centre"* | DTI does not accredit service centres. See §1.3 | *"DTI-registered business name"* — if true |
| *"Authorised"* (any brand) | See §1.2 | *"A listed Michelin and BFGoodrich dealer"* — **verified** |

> **⚠️ The single most likely thing a competitor's signboard will say, and the single
> most likely thing a copywriter will copy from it.** `risks-dos-donts.md` and
> `content/copy-notes.md` both prohibit it. **The Customer is right to be sceptical:
> "certified" with no named certifier is a tell for a shop that does not know what it is
> claiming.** Name the body, name the qualification, name the year. Or say nothing.

### 6.3 What EYG *can* honestly say about its people

The verified evidence about EYG's staff is thin but real, and it is better than nothing:

- The shop has run **two Senior Mechanic vacancies** (2025-06-19, 2025-12-26) seeking
  *"Vehicle diagnostics"* and *"Repair & maintenance"*, with *"Tire mechanic background is a
  plus"* — evidence of a **diagnostic-capable** workshop, and of a real hiring process.
- The shop has **documented and photographed** work that requires competence: a
  **steering rack assembly replacement**, an **AGM battery installation**, brake work and
  alignment, OBD scanning, and a **Senior Mechanic** role.
- It has **documented real fault-finding on a 265/65R17 truck/SUV tyre** and a
  **11R22.5 commercial size** — a tyre retail operation handling sizes that most small
  shops cannot.

**That last point is a genuine and specific differentiator and nobody in the local field
is claiming it.** Write about **capability demonstrated in documented work**, not about
**certification you cannot evidence.** That is the honest version of a trust signal, and
in a market where everyone says "certified", it is far more persuasive.

**`trust.technicians` and `trust.bays` in `site.ts` must stay at `0` / unset.** Not one
technician or bay count is verified.

## 8. NPC registration — what EYG actually has to do

| **Processing sensitive personal information of 1,000 or more individuals** | ❌ A Balanga shop opening 2025-12 would not have 1,000 customers' sensitive data in under a year. **Re-verify annually** |

> IRR, issuances, and circulars**."*

### 8.2 EYG's actual obligations

| # | Obligation | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🔴 **File a notarised Sworn Declaration and Undertaking (Annex 1 of NPC Circular 2022-04)** | *"A PIC or PIP who will not elect voluntary registration is required to file a duly notarized sworn declaration and undertaking."* **This is EYG's real compliance step.** The FAQ also notes it is *"legally binding"* and **no certificate of exemption is issued.** Needs: notarised DPO appointment document + **DTI Certificate of Registration** (sole prop) or **SEC COR + GIS** (corporation) + **valid business permit** |
